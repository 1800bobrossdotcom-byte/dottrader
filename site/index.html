What changed, and why

The first strategy did not work. This is the arithmetic that killed it and what replaced it, published for the same reason the fills are.

01 — What went wrong

The DOT pool charges more per trade than the trade was worth.

The plan was to sell thin slices of DOT into strength and buy them back lower, keeping the difference in DOT. That needs the price gap to beat the cost of trading. The Uniswap V4 DOT/ETH pool runs a dynamic-fee hook charging about 1.1% per swap — 2.188% for a round trip. Every sell had to be bought back more than 2.2% cheaper just to break even, before gas.

Then DOT went to an all-time high while the grid was sitting on sold slices. The buy-backs never triggered, the slices stayed open, and closing them meant buying DOT back dearer than it was sold. That is the whole loss, and it is a real one:

02 — What was checked before changing course

There is no cheaper venue for DOT on Base.

Before abandoning the strategy, every DOT pool on Base was read on chain. One holds meaningful liquidity — about $1.1m at that 1.1% fee. The next largest holds $1,067. There is nowhere else to route, so the fee is not a setting to tune; it is the floor. Round-tripping DOT on Base cannot be made profitable at these fees, so the DOT bots were stopped rather than tuned.

The DOT itself was not sold. It is held in the two trading wallets, listed below, and can be checked on chain.

03 — What replaced it

Earn ETH where trading is cheap, then spend it on DOT into dips.

The same buy-low sell-high logic works on pairs whose pools charge 0.01%–0.05% per swap, between twenty and a hundred times cheaper than the DOT pool. The engine holds ETH, buys a pair after it dips, sells it back after it recovers, and keeps the ETH difference. DOT is never read or spent by that process; the ETH it earns is what buys more DOT later.

It watches four markets against a single budget, because one market produced only two or three signals a day — far too few to ever tell whether the edge is real. The first market to dip past the threshold gets the money.

04 — What this is not

No pair here has a proven edge.

The backtest that justified this returned +32.4% when fills were assumed at the best price inside each candle. Re-run with pessimistic fills — the prices a poller actually sees — the same strategy returned −30.3% on one pair and −0.3% on another, and the trade count fell by roughly six times. The honest reading is that the edge, if any, is much smaller than the first number suggested and the sample is far too thin to size it.

So the budget is deliberately small, it is capped by a hard stop-loss, and the DOT stack is not exposed to it. Four markets exist to gather enough trades to measure this properly. The result of that measurement is published here either way.

How it works

Everything is still scored in DOT. What changed is where the DOT is meant to come from.

01 — EARN (RUNNING)

Swing cheap pairs for ETH.

One budget watches four pools charging 0.01–0.05% a swap. It buys after a pair dips past the threshold, sells once it recovers past it, and keeps the ETH. Every price comes straight from the pool, and the fee is read live because these pools price it dynamically.

02 — HOLD (RUNNING)

The DOT stack sits still.

The DOT grid is stopped, not tuned: a 2.188% round-trip fee cannot be traded through, and no other DOT pool on Base has the liquidity to route around it. The DOT is held in the two trading wallets and is not exposed to the swing budget.

03 — CONVERT (NOT YET)

ETH becomes DOT on dips.

The point of earning ETH is to spend it on DOT when DOT is weak, not on a schedule. That step is deliberately not automated yet: there is not enough closed swing history to justify sizing it, and this page will say so until there is.
