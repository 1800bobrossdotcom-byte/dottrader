The short version. Dot is a barter board. Listing, matching, offers and messages are free, and always will be. Two optional extras carry a small flat fee: completing an NFT-for-NFT swap on chain, and protecting a trade with a card hold. Trades are between you and the other trader; Dot keeps the record, runs the clock and holds the bond. You must be 18 or over to use a card or a wallet here.

1. What Dot is

Dot Trading Post ("Dot", "the board", "we") is a venue where people trade things for other things. We do not own, hold, inspect, ship or insure the items traded. A trade is an agreement between the two traders; Dot records it, keeps the ship-by clock, checks what can be checked (tracking numbers, blockchain transactions) and shows each trader's record to everyone else.

By using the board you agree to these terms. If you don't agree, don't use the board.

3. Trade protection and bonds

On an agreed trade that isn't an on-chain swap, either trader can choose to put up a bond. A bond is a hold on your card for the bond plus the fee ($28.00 today), placed through Stripe. It is a hold, not a charge: nothing is taken until one of the outcomes below.

How it ends

  • The trade completes (both sides press their dot): the fee is kept and the rest of the hold is released.
  • The trade is called off by either side before anyone has marked sent: the hold is released in full, no fee.
  • One side never sends. The ship-by date passes with one trader having marked their side sent and the other not. The trader who sent can close the trade as a no-show. The other trader's whole hold is captured, and the bond amount, less the $2.50 handling fee, is owed to the trader who sent. The trader who sent pays no fee; if their fee had already been taken, it is refunded.

The clock

Card holds last seven days from the moment the card is authorised; that is a limit set by card networks, not by Dot. The ship-by date on every trade is four days after it was agreed. Three things follow:

  • Once you have marked your side sent, your bond has done its job, so on day six the fee is taken and the rest of your hold is released. You'll be emailed when that happens.
  • If the other trader hasn't sent by the ship-by date, close the no-show before their hold lapses. The board shows the date and emails you. After the hold lapses there is nothing to capture and nothing to pay out.
  • A hold that lapses with nobody acting is simply gone: no fee, no payout.

What a bond is

When you put up a bond you and the other trader are agreeing, in advance, that a side which never sends owes the other side the bond amount for the failed trade: the time, postage and lost opportunity of a trade that didn't happen. It is not a fine and it is not a payment to Dot. Dot keeps only the fee and the handling fee listed above.

Reviews, mistakes and disputes

  • Before a forfeit is paid out, someone running the board looks at it. We may ask either trader for the tracking number, the receipt or photos. A no-show that turns out to be wrong (the item was sent and the proof is good) is reversed.
  • If you think a fee was taken or a bond forfeited by mistake, write to us within 7 days through the board. We'd much rather fix it than see a chargeback; a chargeback on a bond you agreed to may close your account.
  • You must be 18 or over, and the cardholder, to put up a bond.

4. Payouts

A forfeited bond is paid to the trader who sent through Stripe Connect. Stripe will ask you for the details it needs to pay you (identity, a bank account), and Stripe's own terms apply to that. Payouts are in US dollars. We approve payouts after review, usually within a day. If a payout can't be delivered to you we will try to reach you by email; an amount that stays unclaimed will be handled under the unclaimed-property rules that apply to it.

5. NFTs and wallets

  • Linking a wallet means signing a message; it is free, moves nothing, and lets every viewer's browser check for itself that you control the address. You can link more than one wallet and unlink any of them.
  • An NFT-for-NFT swap on the same chain goes through Seaport, a public swap contract: both NFTs move in one transaction, or neither does. Dot never holds either NFT. The swap fee is written into the order you sign, and the board refuses an order that asks for more.
  • An NFT sent to the other trader directly is checked on the chain itself: the board confirms that exact token reached one of their linked wallets.
  • Your wallet, its keys and its gas are your own. Dot cannot reverse anything on a blockchain.

6. Your record

Your dots, your completed trades and any no-shows against you are shown on your profile. They are the point of the board. We don't remove a record that is true. A no-show that was recorded by mistake is reversed when the proof is good (see section 3).

7. Listings and conduct

  • List only what you own and can send. Describe it honestly; say what's wrong with it.
  • No stolen, counterfeit or illegal items, and nothing you aren't allowed to sell or send where you or the other trader live.
  • One account per person. Don't put up a bond with someone else's card.
  • We can remove a listing, a message or an account that breaks these rules or that we reasonably believe is being used to deceive someone, and we can refuse to pay out a forfeit that was engineered.

8. Privacy

What we store. Your email address and sign-in; your profile (name, area, note, language, and a location only if you choose to share one); your listings and photos; offers and the messages between you and the other trader; linked wallet addresses with their signatures; trade records including tracking numbers and transaction hashes you enter; bond and payout records (amounts, status and Stripe's identifiers, never card numbers); and which emails we've sent you. Visits are counted as a daily hash of your network address and browser that can't be turned back into either.

Who sees what. Listings, profiles, dots and no-show records are public. Messages are visible only to the two traders in that trade and to us. Wallet addresses you link are public, because every viewer checks them on chain.

Who helps us run it. Supabase (database and sign-in), Vercel (the website), Stripe (card holds and payouts; your card details go to Stripe and never pass through Dot), Resend (email), and public blockchain nodes for on-chain checks, which see the usual connection details when your browser asks them. We don't sell your data and we don't run advertising.

Your choices. You can edit your profile, unlink wallets, switch each kind of email off, and remove your listings at any time. To delete your account, message us through the board and we'll remove it; trade records that other traders' profiles depend on are kept in a form that no longer identifies you.

  • We are not a party to any trade and are not responsible for what a trader sends, doesn't send, or says. Where the law lets us, our liability to you is limited to the fees you have paid us in the twelve months before the claim.

  • Questions, mistakes, or anything that doesn't feel right: write to us through the board, or reply to any email the board has sent you.
